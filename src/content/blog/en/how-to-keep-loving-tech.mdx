## I turned 30 this year...

thinking back, I've been coding for almost half of my life. I try to remember
what got me in there in the first place. I'm not so proud of it but it was
actually writing cheats and bots for my favorite MMO at that time lol. Nah
writing this I'm proud and I regret nothing. The choices I made at that time,
they got me there. Thanks to this path, I married my wife, I'm able to provide
for my family, and I can just live a peaceful life.

One of the key aspects of who I am is pretty much tied to the fact that I'm a
tech guy. I fucking love tech, I wake up tech, I live tech, I breathe tech. I
fell in love with coding because it allows me to stay close to the tech; solving
weird problems by doing tech stuff. The truth is that most of the time, I was
more interested in fixing the problem than in understanding the problem itself.
Not sure if this makes sense but, I was excited by the idea of waking up in the
morning and doing some weird hard shit with code to make managers and
stakeholders happy.

I'm a people person, and I was lowkey getting off when explaining the hard shit
that I was doing to other people. My goal was to enable them to do the same shit
without me having to help them the next time. And I was pretty good at it. I was
not one of those mfs on StackOverflow telling folks how much they were stupid
for asking about things. I was encouraging people to ask questions, first
because I genuinely wanted to help, and second because **it made me feel good
about myself**. I don't think it's necessarily like a bad thing tho. Everybody
wants to be known for something and this was _my_ thing.

This is why I started streaming by the way. Talking about code, sharing my code,
debating about code, learning about code, everything code. Writing this, I
really feel like a fucking nerd (derogatory), but you have to understand that
this shit is like MUSIC to my ears.

I'm so lucky to be who I am. HUGE nerd but also a big people person. This is why
I was given all the opportunities I had. Not only because of my skills, but also
because of luck and because I'm damn nice (and funny). Thanks to this, I met so
many people, worked for some of the smartest folks on earth (shoutout to my man
Bhaskar) and was able to make a shitton of money. So much money that I can't
tell it to my dad (I'm Congolese, you wouldn't understand).

And yet, here I am, writing about being bored now.

## AI is a wonderful tool,

because they (frontier labs) took all of our content, the good, the bad, the
weird stuff, added the maths to it, and now you can get EVERYTHING from a
chatbox (you obviously have to pay for it, you don't own shit little peasant).
We initially tried to cope so hard with some of the dumbest shit you've ever
seen, like:

> UNIVERSAL BASIC INCOME

> MORE TIME FOR CREATIVE WORK

> NEW OPPORTUNITIES FOR EVERYONE

> WE'RE ABOUT TO SPEND LESS TIME AT WORK

but deep down we knew, or I knew, that AI was first coming for the joy I was
getting from my day-to-day work. When you're a slave to the capital, the goal is
efficiency. If you get happiness from it, that's an unexpected (happy) side
effect but your job is primarily to get the job done as fast, as sound, and as
secure as possible. And, even if nobody wants to admit it, AI is about that. It
is about getting to the point as fast as you can fucking possibly go. This, at
scale, will do irreversible damage to the world as we know it but hey, I'm not
talking about that today. I'm talking about the fact that all the parts I was
REALLY enjoying about my job are gone and this is pretty bad for me because I
don't want to do shit, if I don't enjoy it. I'm speaking from privilege here
folks, I was able to tell people that I REALLY love my job and I meant it. Not
anymore!

So what now? What am I doing? What's the solution?

Well, I seriously started questioning myself like a year ago, mid 2025, when I
got really negative about the future. Not a doomer but almost. Talked to my
wife, talked to my friends, even some coworkers I trust the most and kept asking
the same question. What now? What am I? What's left for me?

The first thing was that, I have to accept that things are not going backward
with AI. It's here to stay whether I like it or not. The best I can do is find
ways to have fun with it and the thing that clicked for me is this thing kids
are calling harness engineering. It's basically the same thinking process I had
when I started coding: how can I automate some bullshit that I don't want to do
manually. This is a whole set of new problems and abstractions. It's less
fulfilling in a way because there is no determinism in here. You throw random
bullshit and see what sticks to the wall BUT, the deeper you go, the more you
start seeing some patterns and ways to bend the Machine God to your will. This
has been really interesting to work on, from a tech perspective and I've found
myself enjoying sharing my journey while working on this new class of problems.

The next thing is that technically, outside of my work, NOTHING is preventing me
from coding. So whenever I have some spare time, I just code. I've been building
some shit recently, with more or less AI assistance and it has been fun. This is
my little secret, like I'm still doing handmade coding. Crazy to talk about this
like this is some kind of ancient arcane art lol.

The last thing is that tech is much more than coding. I've been stuck in this
rabbit hole because my career was tied to it but I can do so much more. 3D
printing, electronics, hardware... You'd say yeah, AI's coming for that as well
and you're right but hey, this is kind of new to me and it's broadening my
perspective. A project I can do isn't just code now, it's a system. I'm
basically saying that AI made me go out and touch grass in a way lol.

---

This one is for my nerds. There's hope out there. We can still build, thrive,
try stuff, break stuff. You just have to accept that professionally, the
goalpost has moved. But what you fell in love with, is still there. We still
have communities, we still have people who want to learn, ask questions, and
people who still care. It's not over.
