Before we start, a little disclaimer. Most of the things mentioned here are
expensive. This is not my fault; it's Sam Altman's. You always have to pay a
price. I'm in a position where I can pay with money instead of my data!

## Why?

My friends always hit me with that question whenever I come up with new shit I'm
trying to push on them. Being my friend can be annoying sometimes. It's always
about some hot shit to test, something we need to migrate to, something we need
to save for. But the cool thing is that you'll always know about trends and
niche stuff. I'm #that guy I guess.

Trump got elected like 2 years ago (feels like 12 to be honest). Man has said a
lot of bullshit, but one of the things that kind of stuck with me is when he
lowkey (highkey?) threatened EU tech sovereignty. You, as the reader, being a
smartass, would think "well, why do u care? you're Canadian now" so let me get
to the point. My digital life depends on closed-source US services. I'm an Apple
fanboy; my email, calendar, and life depend on Apple, Google, Netflix,
Microsoft, Nvidia... you name it, every tech company out there. Tech CEOs cannot
be trusted, so if anything happens, my access can be cut off for whatever
reason.

> This is something I always knew deep down. The truth is that, until now,
> trading ownership for comfort felt good.

## The easy way

If you look into it, it's almost impossible to totally opt out. Alternatives
don't exist or are straight-up dog shit. But if you dig, you can find some
hidden (?) gems out there. It always comes at a cost, but this is what it takes
to try to keep some kind of privacy and agency. I can name some, and y'all will
inevitably complain about how private, how secure, or how anonymous this shit
is, but hey, baby steps (for some reason, some people are insufferable when you
don't mention their weird-ass tool). Nothing has to be perfect from the get-go.
Dropping some categories and alternatives here for lazy asses.

| Base service     | Alternatives                                                           |
| ---------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Search engine    | DuckDuckGo, Ecosia (meh), Qwant (Euroslop), Brave Search               |
| Web browser      | Helium (Goat), Zen (SpiderMonkey slow), Brave (Crypto slop), LibreWolf |
| Email & Calendar | Proton, Tuta, Atomic Mail, Fastmail                                    |
| Drive & Photos   | Proton Drive (no Linux client, I made a custom one), Internxt, Filen   |
| Video streaming  | Jellyfin (you have to source your own shit)                            |

These are some examples. Reddit, Discord servers, and nerds will do a way better
job than me of showing you all the alternatives, but this is where I want you to
start looking. If you're a criminal looking for a way to get away, I can't help
you. Almost everything will snitch on you to the highest governmental authority.
But at least it's not private companies snooping in your stuff!

## The hard way

This is what I'm starting to do now. If you want something done well, like you'd
do it for someone else, the only way is to do it yourself!

> SELF-HOSTING!

The act of getting a machine, finding a way to expose it to the internet, and
putting some stuff on it so you can access it. Now, should you self-host
everything? Some people will say yeah, others won't. My point is, it's way
EASIER than one would think. All you need is a brain and a little bit of tech
literacy.

Like with everything, there are levels to this stuff. You can go with a consumer
product that will give you a server, some hard drives, and a plug-and-play type
of experience, like the
[Zima Cube](https://www.zimaspace.com/products/cube-personal-cloud?pos=nav_zc2),
[UGreen products](https://www.ugreen.com/en-ca/collections/nas?from=nav_all_button),
or
[Synology products](https://www.synology.com/en-ca/products?product_line=ds_j%2Cds_plus%2Cds_value%2Cds_xs).
They are a great (expensive) way to start this journey. My most tech-illiterate
(rich) friends are running some of them in a basement somewhere and enjoying it.

If you want a LEARNING experience in networking, hardware, and infrastructure,
you can follow me and get some old, cheap, perfectly fine hardware. Dell
OptiPlex, Lenovo ThinkCentre, HP EliteDesk—there are entire subreddits dedicated
to them. It's amazing what you can achieve with them. This is what I'm doing,
and this is what I'll be documenting next year. I'm getting cheap, recycled
hardware and building stuff on top of it for me and my family.

I've already started, by the way. Got stuff like Proxmox, OPNsense, and AdGuard
running, and I'll slowly but surely add services, hard drives, more hardware...

Sam Altman doesn't want you to have compute power. Zuck wants all your data. But
me, Chris, I want you to be FREE!!!!!!
